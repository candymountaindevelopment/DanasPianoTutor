About Danas Ear

Danas Ear listens to the microphone and shows the pitch it hears: note name, how many cents sharp or flat, and the frequency in hertz. It is a companion to Danas Piano Tutor; the note log is the raw material for comparing what a student played with a lesson.

How it works

  • The browser's Web Audio API delivers the microphone signal in 4096-sample blocks.
  • Each block goes through the YIN algorithm (de Cheveigné & Kawahara, 2002): a cumulative-mean normalised difference function with parabolic interpolation, which finds the fundamental period even when a harmonic is louder than the fundamental.
  • Clarity is 1 minus the YIN difference at the chosen period — near 1 for a clean tone, low for noise. Frames below the level gate or with clarity under 0.6 are ignored.
  • A note event starts when the same note is heard in three consecutive frames and ends after four silent or different frames (~100 ms). The frequency shown for an event is the median of its frames.

Tips

  • Piano notes below C2 and above C7 are outside the detector range (40–2000 Hz fundamental).
  • Chords cannot be separated: the detector reports one pitch, usually the lowest strong one.
  • Use Play tone to check the setup without a microphone; the tone is analysed the same way.

TUTOR + EAR, ONE SCREEN

Two apps, two toolbars and four tabs become one screen with three modes.

Nothing was removed. Everything that was a tab, a panel or a second page is now either on the screen, one keystroke away, or drawn on the thing it describes — the fingering on the keys, the heard pitch under the staff.

WHAT NEVER MOVES

The staff sits in the same place in Learn and Practice. The keyboard never moves in any mode. Switching mode slides the tuner ribbon in and changes the right panel — that is all. There is nothing behind a tab you have to remember.

Two toolbars, 23 controls One bar: the mode you are in, ⌘K, Listen, and a ⋯ menu. Open, Save, MusicXML, WAV, Print and Share live in the palette.
A lesson list sitting in the side panel The engraved title is the switcher. Click it and the lessons in the document drop down under it.
A Settings tab competing with the lesson Three modes on the rail — Learn, Practice, Ear — with the 30 feature switches at its foot, where a teacher sets them once.
Four tabs: Lesson, Script, Practice, Settings Two, in the top bar itself: Lesson and Practice. Script is ⌘E and opens over the screen, because writing a lesson is not playing one.
A note lane as tall as the score — a third view of the same nine notes A 74 px tape on the staff's own x‑axis, so a bar lines up with the bar above it. Off by default for readers.
Two hand diagrams in boxes, away from the keys The hands are drawn on the keyboard, on the keys they are holding, with the finger numbers on the keys themselves.
Danas Ear: a second page, in a second tab, with its own keyboard A mode. The tuner ribbon slides in under the staff; in Ear the trace takes the staff's place. Same keyboard, same clock, one microphone.
